Lesson 3 · 20 min

3-phase approval in a small business

In a small business, the assistant doesn't just start sending messages on its own. It climbs a ladder: first the owner approves everything, then only the important things, and finally only the exceptions. Each step takes control away from the owner only where the record shows it's safe, and always with a way back.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1What changes in each of the 3 phases
  2. 2Write the "important" table with the owner
  3. 3Classify each action as low, medium or high risk
  4. 4Practice the conversation where the owner wants to skip steps

Goal: turn the assistant on in phase 1, write down with the owner what counts as "important," and agree in writing on when to move up and when to go back a phase.

01 · The approval ladder

Climb one step at a time, with the record in hand. Tap each phase.

Serious error, or the owner feels unsure? Go back one phase the same day, no blame.
Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

The owner decides when to move up. You bring the record and make the proposal.

02 · What counts as "important" belongs to the clinic

There is no universal list. Build it with the owner before turning on phase 2.

1. Show

Pick about 20 recent messages from the phase 1 record, with no names or phone numbers.

Ask the owner to mark each one: "I wanted to see it" or "it could have gone out on its own."

2. Find the pattern

Look at what the ones marked "I wanted to see it" have in common: a word ("discount", "cancel"), a type of patient, a time of day, a long voice message.

Each pattern becomes a row in the table.

3. Write it together

The table goes into the playbook, in the limits section, with the date.

The owner approves in writing: a message saying "agreed" is enough as a record.

4. Review

At each phase change and, as a rule of thumb, every month, look at what went to the queue without needing to and what went straight through and shouldn't have.

If almost everything becomes "important," the owner never gets to rest; if almost nothing does, she loses control.

03 · Three risk levels

Every action the assistant takes gets a label in the playbook. More levels than that turns into theory.

1. Low

Greeting, a question from the list, business hours, an out-of-hours notice.

Can reach phase 3.

2. Medium

Booking request, confirmation of an appointment already booked, rescheduling at no cost.

Moves up to phase 2 or 3 with a weekly look at a sample.

3. High

Package price, discount, cancellation with a fee, complaint, question about pain or medication.

Always stays in phase 1: a person approves before it goes out.

Connection to agent managementIt's the same idea as the green, yellow, and red authority levels in the AI Management · Agents course, in a front-desk version.

"I'll turn on phase 2 on <day> at <time> and let you know when it's on."

    06 · Practice

    Use the assistant and the playbook from lessons 1 and 2.

    1 · Board of the 3 phases and risk labels

    30 min

    Fill in the phase board and label each assistant action as low, medium, or high risk in the playbook.

    Done when: the board has the 3 phases with what goes out directly in each one, every playbook action has a risk label, and the high-risk actions are in the "never leaves phase 1" row.

    2 · "Important" table and phase rule

    30 min

    Build the "important" table from messages (real ones with names removed, or the Vila Serena ones) and write the rule for moving up and moving back a phase, with the numbers you and the owner agree on.

    Done when: the table has at least 6 rows with "goes to the queue: yes/no", the phase rule is marked as "consultant's rule, agreed" and has the condition for going back and who decides.
