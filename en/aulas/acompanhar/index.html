Lesson 6 · 17 min

Follow up: 20-minute check-in, 5 indicators, 3 alerts

Without numbers, in a bad week the owner's impression becomes "it's not working." With numbers, you show what has changed since the baseline. Follow-up is a short, fixed ritual: 20 minutes at the same time, five numbers, one adjustment, and a one-page report. And three signals that call for a visit, not a discount.

A short, fixed ritual holds the clinic together better than a long meeting when something breaks.

02 · The 5 reception indicators

Five, not thirty. Collected in a few minutes by the champion, checked by you. No ready-made target: the comparison is with the number from before.

1. Replies

How many messages the assistant prepared or answered. It shows whether it's being used.

On its own it misleads: lots of volume with lots of editing is not a gain.

2. Time

Time between the patient's message and the first reply, during service hours.

In phase 1 it includes approval time: that's what shows whether the queue is weighing things down.

3. No reply

Contacts that ended the day with no reply at all. It's the metric from the lesson 1 sheet at Vila Serena (fictional).

It's the number that shows patients lost to delay.

4. Bookings

Appointments booked that started in a conversation with the assistant, checked against the schedule.

It shows whether the conversation turns into an appointment, not just a reply.

5. Hours

The champion's estimate of how much reception time was freed up during the week, stated as an estimate.

It's the number the owner feels; that's why it goes with how it was counted written down.

BaselineEach indicator needs the value from before, measured the same way and in the same kind of week. Without it, the report shows movement, not results. For a deep dive on measuring 30/60/90: Professional 2030 · Consultant, lesson 6.

03 · The 3 red flags

Any one of them calls for a visit, a consultant's rule of thumb: in person if possible, within two days, free of charge.

1. Champion disappeared

Canceled two check-ins in a row, stopped filling in the log, or doesn't answer messages, even while saying everything is fine.

Often it's overload or an exit in sight: go back to the sheet of roles.

2. Number dropped

An indicator plunged to near or below the baseline for no reason (holiday, vacation, schedule change).

Usage quietly dropping often comes before quitting.

3. "I'll think about it"

"I'm deciding whether to continue," "it's weighing on our cash," "I'll check with the team."

When this phrase shows up, the conversation needs to happen face to face, and soon.

ResponseListen before defending the system, ask directly "is something bothering you that I'm not seeing?" and leave with a two-week plan. A discount right away doesn't fix the reason.

04 · The cadence that shrinks

Follow-up loosens as the clinic matures. Timeframes are a rule of thumb, adjusted case by case.

1. Start

In the first weeks, a check-in every week: the clinic is still unsure and the playbook is still changing.

2. Routine

With phase 2 stable and few new questions, every two weeks, with a short message in the week without a meeting.

3. Stable

Stable phase and a confident champion: one conversation a month and a monthly report in place of the weekly one. This is where follow-up turns into maintenance (lesson 7).

4. Crisis

New employee, change of service, red alert: back to weekly until things stabilize again.

08 · Summary

Fixed ritual20 minutes, same day and time: numbers, questions and adjustments, next week.
5 numbersAlways next to the baseline; the trend matters more than the week's number.
3 alertsChampion goes quiet, a number falling with no explanation, and "I'll think about it": visit and listen, no discount.
CadenceWeekly, every two weeks, monthly; in a crisis, back to weekly.
