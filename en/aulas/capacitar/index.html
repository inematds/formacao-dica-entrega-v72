Lesson 5 · 17 min

Train: the owner, the internal champion, and the 90-minute training

An assistant with no one inside looking after it becomes an orphan: everything built, nothing sustaining it. In a small business, that someone is the owner or a trusted person. Choosing that person is part of the delivery, and the 90-minute training, the one-page manual, and the question log are what let the clinic get along without you.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Choose the champion with 3 questions
  2. 2Split the roles on one sheet
  3. 3Give the 90-minute training hands-off
  4. 4Keep the manual and the question log alive

Goal: choose the internal champion, lead the 90-minute training, and get the 1-page manual and the question log up and running.

01 · From choosing the champion to the question log

Five pieces, in this order. Tap each one.

Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

Training is part of the delivery. Without a champion, the assistant is orphaned the first time an employee changes.

02 · The right champion

A consultant's rule of thumb, drawn from experience with the earlier DICA material.

1. 3 questions

"Who will look after the assistant every day, by name?" "What are the odds this person is still here next year?" "Do they know they'll be responsible, and did they agree?"

If any answer is weak, the owner is the champion until someone better comes along.

2. Right

Right: a permanent person, trusted by the owner to change an answer, with time set aside in their schedule for it.

Wrong: someone who just arrived, someone who already juggles too many roles, someone assigned without being asked, someone afraid of being replaced.

3. Illustrative case

A clinic chose a newly hired receptionist as its champion. She used it well for the first few weeks, then said she was leaving, and nobody trained her replacement.

With no one looking after it, the answers went stale, and the clinic concluded that "the AI didn't work." The problem was the champion, not the assistant.

SafeguardThe roles sheet says what happens when the champion leaves: a new 90-minute training with whoever steps in, before the person walks out the door.

03 · The 90-minute training

Preferably in person, on the clinic's own computer and phone.

1. 0 to 30

Open the queue and explain each part in the clinic's own words, no jargon.

Show one whole case: the message arrives, the assistant prepares a reply, someone approves, the reply goes out, it lands in the log.

2. 30 to 60

The champion does everything on her own. You watch with your hands in your pockets, even when she makes mistakes.

Write down the real questions that come up: they start the question log.

3. 60 to 90

Answers, one adjustment made in front of her, and the manual in hand.

The training only ends with the first check-in booked, with a fixed day and time.

Sign of good trainingShe did everything on her own in the second third and left with two or three real questions. "Everything's fine" with no questions at all is a sign she didn't learn.

04 · The material that stays

Little, visible, and up to date.

1. Manual

A printed page near whoever answers customers is worth more than a file lost on the computer.

Update it when the screen or the way of approving changes.

2. Log

Date, what happened, context, and what was done. A simple spreadsheet or a pinned chat on your phone.

No patient names or phone numbers: describe the case, not the person.

3. 3 of the same

When the same question comes up three times, it becomes an adjustment to the playbook or the manual, not just a one-off answer.

If you serve more than one clinic in the same niche, it becomes a piece of your library (lesson 7).

4. Recording

Record the screen or keep screenshots of real conversations to train other people only with the owner's written permission and with names, phone numbers, and brand hidden.

A patient's conversation is personal data and, at a clinic, it can be health data (LGPD, Brazil's data protection law).

06 · Practice

For Vila Serena (fictional) or for the business you serve.

1 · Champion, roles, and training script

40 min

Answer the champion's 3 questions, fill in the roles sheet, and adapt the 90-minute training script to your clinic's assistant.

Done when: the champion has a name and the 3 questions have answers, the sheet has the 4 rows with deadlines and what happens if the champion leaves, and the script has the 3 thirds with at least 3 tasks she does on her own.

2 · Manual and question log

30 min

Write the one-page manual and open the question log with the questions that came up in training (or 3 likely questions).

Done when: the manual fits on one page, has 4 to 6 steps, how to stop everything, the emergency contact, and at least 3 common questions; the log has the 4 columns and 3 rows with no patient names or phone numbers.
